How to Ensure New Patients Can Find Your Clinic Online

SKED • May 2026 • 7 min read

Most new patients don't find their chiropractor through a referral anymore. They find them on Google. They check reviews, scroll through a website, and make a decision before they ever pick up the phone. For chiropractic practices, that means your digital presence isn't a nice-to-have. It's your first impression, your front door, and your most active sales tool.

Word-of-mouth has always been the foundation of chiropractic growth. Today, it lives online. If your practice isn't showing up and looking credible when patients go searching, you're losing them to a competitor who is.

Here's how to make sure new patients can actually find you and choose you when they do.

Show up on Social Media

Younger patients increasingly start their search for healthcare providers on social media rather than Google. An inactive or inconsistent social presence tells prospective patients the practice isn't engaged, and engagement is a proxy for trust.

Keeping your channels active doesn't require a full content team. Consistent posting, responding to comments and messages, and sharing content that reflects your practice's personality and expertise goes a long way. Think patient education, behind-the-scenes moments, and service spotlights that connect with your existing patients and attract new ones.

SKED's automated patient engagement tools keep your practice top of mind between visits, making it easier to stay connected with your community without adding to your team's workload.

Get Serious About Local SEO

Local SEO is one of the highest-leverage investments a chiropractic practice can make. According to recent research, 92% of people never click past page one of Google search results. If your practice isn't showing up there, it effectively doesn't exist for the majority of people searching for a chiropractor in your area.

Online reviews directly influence where you show up. Reviews contribute roughly 13% of Google's local search ranking factors, which means practices that actively manage their reputation — including their Google Business profile, website content, and review volume — rank higher and capture more local traffic. Consistent visibility in search and a strong online reputation compound over time into a significant patient growth engine.

Chiropractic practices using SKED's automated review requests receive on average two to four times more Google reviews within 90 days, directly strengthening local search rankings and building the social proof that converts browsers into booked appointments.

Make Your Website Work Hard

Your website is where curiosity turns into a booked appointment. Most practices struggle to estimate the effectiveness of their websites. A well-built chiropractic website needs to cover a few non-negotiables.

A complete rundown of your services matters more than most practices realize, especially your differentiation of services and technology. If you offer decompression therapy, laser therapy, or other specialized care, your website needs to say so clearly. Patients are searching for specific solutions, and a generic service page won't capture them.

Patient testimonials and reviews need to be easy to find. Don't bury them at the bottom of a page no one scrolls to. Social proof placed prominently throughout your site accelerates trust and pushes prospective patients toward booking.

Similarly, online booking is a strong conversion tool. Patients who want to book outside of business hours or simply prefer not to call will leave your site without scheduling if booking isn't available. SPARK helps optimize website conversions so more of the traffic your SEO and reputation efforts drive actually turns into new patient enrollment.

Treat Google Reviews as an Operational Priority

The 2026 State of the Chiropractic Industry Report shows 63.5% of practice growth still comes from personal referrals, making your online reputation the thing that either reinforces or undermines every recommendation you receive. Today, word of mouth doesn't stay in face-to-face conversations. It shows up on Google, where prospective patients are already searching, and your review profile is often the first referral they encounter.

A practice with a weak or sparse review profile loses 10 to 20 new patients every single month to competitors with stronger digital validation, even when the quality of care is identical. When review volume is low, the cost compounds invisibly. Fewer clicks from search results lead to fewer calls and fewer bookings. Even strong referrals will scroll past a listing to choose a competitor with more visible trust.

Despite growing patient volume being the top priority for most chiropractic practices, the report found 28% of clinics have fewer than 25 Google reviews. That lack of social proof acts like a quiet leak in a practice's potential, and nearly 50% of clinics spend less than $500 per month on marketing to compensate. The solution isn't always to spend more. It's to fix the digital footprint.

The 300 Review Threshold

In most markets, reaching 300 Google reviews secures a spot among the top five most-reviewed chiropractors in your city. That visibility creates a conversion engine that works on autopilot, generating 10 to 20 new patients monthly through pure organic authority. Getting there requires moving through a specific ladder of credibility.

With 0 to 50 reviews, a practice simply proves the doors are open. With 51 to 150, it earns a seat at the conversation but still loses patients to more established competitors. Reaching 151 to 300 reviews is where momentum shifts.

The practice becomes the safe bet — a low-risk, high-reward choice for prospective patients. At 300 and beyond, a practice stops being a hidden gem and starts being the local authority. New patients find you, trust you, and book with you.

There is another powerful benefit to building reviews at scale. Patients who read hundreds of success stories before their first appointment arrive with a sense of pre-sold confidence. They show higher care plan acceptance, better follow-through, and stronger clinical outcomes. Reviews don't just drive new patients. They improve the quality of the patient relationship from day one.

Why Manual Review Requests Don't Work

Most practices understand that reviews matter. The challenge is execution. In a busy clinic, front desk teams juggle scheduling, insurance verification, check-ins, and provider coordination. The review request is almost always the first thing clinics drop when volume spikes.

Some clinics ask patients immediately after their appointment, but those patients forget by the time they reach the parking lot. Others send a follow-up email days later, long after the momentum of the visit has faded. Most never ask at all. And without a system to identify which patients had positive experiences, every request carries risk. Asking the wrong patient at the wrong moment can turn private dissatisfaction into a public review that pulls down your average rating.

The result is predictable: a practice full of happy patients with very few online reviews to show for it, and a handful of negative ones carrying disproportionate weight.
