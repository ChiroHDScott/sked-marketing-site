10 KPIs High-Growth Chiropractic Practices Actually Track

SKED • April 2026 • 8 min read

Knowing your key performance indicators (KPIs) is a competitive advantage in chiropractic. The practices scaling fastest run their businesses with the same precision they bring to patient care — and it starts with tracking the right metrics. The metrics that drive growth, not just the ones that track activity.

If your only benchmark is "are we busy?", you may be leaving growth opportunities on the table — and you might be busy for the wrong reasons. Busy can come from manual processes and inefficiencies that burn out your staff.

Here are 10 KPIs top-performing practices prioritize, and why each one is essential for the growth of your clinic.

1

New Patient Volume

Most practices are familiar with this one. New patient volume measures how many new patients you bring in each month or quarter. It's one of the most direct indicators of whether your marketing, referral engine, and community presence are actually working.

Tracking this number over time reveals patterns. Volume that dips after a staff change or plateaus through a slow season stops being a surprise when you're watching it consistently. When you know your baseline, you can set real targets and tie them to specific actions — such as a new Google Ads campaign, a referral push, or a community event.

2

Patient Retention Rate

New patients are great, but acquiring them costs money. Retaining the patients you already have is the real secret to building sustainable revenue. Patient retention rate measures the percentage of patients completing their care plans or returning for maintenance care, and it tells you how well your practice delivers on its clinical promise.

A low retention rate signals a need for investigation across both clinical and operational functions. Patient drop-offs mid-plan, gaps in care-plan education, and scheduling friction are all worth examining. High-growth practices treat retention as a priority on both fronts, and the best ones don't manage it manually. 3

No-Show and Cancellation Rate

Every missed appointment is lost revenue. No-show and cancellation rates measure the percentage of missed or canceled appointments, and for most practices, they're metrics that show a significant amount of lost revenue. According to the 2026 State of the Chiropractic Industry Report, the average chiropractic clinic loses $40,000 annually to no-shows alone.

Tracking this number helps you identify trends — including certain days, providers, or appointment types that have higher no-show rates. Next, you can implement systems such as automated reminders, confirmation workflows, and rebooking processes that recover revenue before it walks out the door.

4

Average Revenue Per Patient

Average revenue per patient is calculated by dividing your total revenue by your number of active patients. It tells you how much value you're generating from the patients already in your care. This metric matters because growth doesn't always mean more patients.

Increasing average revenue per patient — just by a little — can have a significant impact on practice profitability, and additional value-added services can translate into better patient outcomes.

5

Lifetime Patient Value (LTV)

Lifetime patient value represents the average total revenue a patient generates across their entire relationship with your clinic. It's a longer view than many practices take. Considering the long play changes how you think about everything from marketing spend to patient experience.

When you know your LTV, you can make smarter decisions about how much to spend to acquire a new patient. You can also start identifying which patient cohorts have the highest LTV and design your marketing plans to focus on niche patient populations who are more likely to stay at your clinic long-term.

6

Referral Volume

Referral volume tracks new patients who came to you through an existing patient or a partner practice. It's one of the strongest signals of patient satisfaction your practice has. People refer to you when they trust you and believe in what you do.

High-growth practices don't leave referrals to chance. They actively solicit referrals, and they track where referrals come from, which providers or staff members generate the most, and whether referral numbers move in response to specific programs or initiatives. Low referral volume is a signal worth taking seriously with your team.

7

Online Reputation Metrics

Your Google presence is often the first impression a prospective patient has of your practice. Online reputation metrics — including total review count, average star rating, and response rate — directly influence whether someone books with you or moves on to the next result.

Top practices treat reviews as an operational function. For high performers, generating reviews isn't a manual process; it's a standard part of business operations. SKED's automated review requests go out at the right moment without anyone on your team having to remember to ask, keeping your Google presence active and your star rating climbing consistently.

8

Utilization of Differentiating Services

If your practice offers decompression therapy, laser therapy, or other specialized cash-pay services, are patients actually using them? Utilization rate measures the percentage of patients who engage with your differentiating services and indicates whether your team is communicating your full scope of care.

Low utilization of a high-value service is usually a communication or workflow issue rather than a demand issue. Tracking this metric gives you the visibility to address it directly.

9

Staff Efficiency Metrics

Growth requires operational capacity. Staff efficiency metrics — including front desk call volume, average check-in time, and percentage of tasks handled automatically — tell you whether your team has the bandwidth to support a growing patient base.

Practices that track efficiency often find that manual, repetitive tasks consume hours that could be devoted to patient experience and revenue-generating activities. Knowing exactly where those bottlenecks live is the first step to eliminating them.

10

Marketing ROI

"Half the money I spend on advertising is wasted; the trouble is I don't know which half."

— John Wanamaker

Marketing spend that can't be measured often results in costly inefficiency. Marketing ROI tracks cost per lead, cost per new patient acquisition, and campaign conversion rates — giving you clarity on which channels and campaigns are actually driving growth.

High-growth practices use those numbers to make decisions fast. They scale what works and cut what doesn't.

Frequently Asked Questions

What KPIs should chiropractic practices track?

The most important KPIs include new patient volume, patient retention rate, no-show and cancellation rate, average revenue per patient, lifetime patient value, referral volume, online reputation metrics, utilization of differentiating services, staff efficiency, and marketing ROI. Tracking these together gives you a complete picture of both clinical and business performance.

How do you calculate patient retention rate for a chiropractic clinic?

Patient retention rate is calculated by dividing the number of patients who completed their care plans (or returned for follow-up care) by the total number of patients who began care in a given period, then multiplying by 100. A high retention rate signals that patients trust your clinical outcomes and that your scheduling and communication workflows support plan compliance.

What is lifetime patient value (LTV) in chiropractic?

Lifetime patient value (LTV) is the average total revenue a patient generates across their entire relationship with your clinic — from their first visit through ongoing maintenance care. Knowing your LTV helps you set smarter patient acquisition budgets, identify your highest-value patient cohorts, and make decisions about marketing spend with confidence.

How can chiropractic practices reduce no-show rates?

The most effective way to reduce no-shows is through automated appointment reminders sent by text and email at key intervals — typically 24 hours and 3 hours before the appointment. Practices that require text confirmation and use automated rebooking workflows for cancellations consistently report no-show reductions of 25–50%.
