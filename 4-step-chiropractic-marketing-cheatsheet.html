The 4-Step Chiropractic Marketing Cheatsheet

Most practices treat marketing like a fire drill — push hard when volume dips, go quiet when things feel steady. That inconsistency is exactly what keeps practices stuck. The practices winning on new patient acquisition and retention have one thing in common: consistency. Here's the framework to make that happen.

1

Step One

Build Your Current Patient Marketing Engine

Your existing patients are your highest-value marketing asset. Automate personalized emails and texts to reactivate lapsed patients and keep current patients engaged. Send regular emails with success stories, tips, and practice updates. Launch a loyalty program to reward repeat visits and keep current patients feeling valued.

2

Step Two

Build Your New Patient Acquisition Engine

Getting new patients through the door requires visibility, social proof, and frictionless booking. Post consistently on social media, run paid local ads, and equip your referral network with materials that link directly to your online booking tool. The easier it is to book, the faster referrals convert.

3

Step Three

Build Your Content and SEO Foundation

Content marketing costs 62% less than traditional marketing and generates three times as many leads. Update your blog regularly with treatment videos, patient tips, and condition-specific content. Send a monthly newsletter to stay top of mind between visits and support your SEO with fresh, locally relevant content.

4

Step Four

Measure, Segment, and Adjust

According to the 2026 State of the Chiropractic Industry Report, 30% of practice owners have no confidence in their ability to track marketing ROI. Marketing without measurement is a cost. Segment campaigns into two buckets — current patients and new patient acquisition — and track cost per lead, cost per new patient, and conversion rates monthly.

The Steady Drumbeat Wins

Pick your channels, build your cadence, automate what you can, and measure everything. Sporadic marketing stalls. Consistent marketing compounds.
